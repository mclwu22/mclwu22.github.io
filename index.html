BrainDINO — a single representation that generalizes across tasks

First author
Self-supervised foundation model · DINOv3-style self-distillation · PyTorch, multi-GPU
6.6Munlabeled images pretrained on
20heterogeneous source datasets
7downstream task families
0task labels used in pretraining

Most learning-based pipelines in medical imaging are task-specific and label-hungry. BrainDINO asks whether one self-supervised representation can cover endpoints that differ in supervision type, spatial granularity, and semantics — from dense segmentation to coarse classification to continuous regression.

  • Pretraining. Self-distillation on 6.6M unlabeled axial slices aggregated from 20 datasets spanning different populations, diseases, scanners, and acquisition protocols.
  • Evaluation protocol. The encoder stays frozen; only lightweight task heads are trained. This isolates representation quality from fine-tuning capacity and makes cross-task comparison honest.
  • Coverage. Tumor segmentation, neurodegenerative and neurodevelopmental classification, brain-age estimation, post-stroke temporal prediction, molecular status prediction, sequence classification, and survival modeling.
  • Result. Matches or exceeds both natural-image pretraining and domain-specific SSL baselines across tasks, with the largest margins under label scarcity — the regime that actually matters in deployment.
  • Analysis. CKA and representation-similarity studies show anatomically organized, pathology-sensitive feature structure emerging without any task supervision.

Why it transfers to recommendation: this is scaling-law-driven representation learning on messy, heterogeneous, mostly-unlabeled data — the same problem shape as learning user, product, and content embeddings from behavioral logs, where labels are sparse and the encoder has to serve many downstream heads at once.

Multimodal fusion under missing modalities

Research
Cross-attention fusion · survival & risk modeling · cross-site generalization

Real cohorts are ragged: some inputs are missing, some sites differ systematically from others. I built fusion architectures that combine imaging representations with structured clinical variables, and tested them where it hurts — with modalities dropped at inference, and with train/test split across institutions.

  • Cross-attention and cascade fusion designs benchmarked against published baselines under a fixed, pre-registered split protocol.
  • Explicit missing-modality handling rather than complete-case filtering, audited end to end.
  • Cross-site transfer (train on one institution's cohort, evaluate on another) as the primary generalization test.
  • Time-to-event heads (DeepHit-style) for risk stratification, not just classification accuracy.

Why it transfers: heterogeneous data fusion and alignment is the same core challenge as unifying user behavior, sales time series, and multimodal product content into one representation — including the unglamorous part, where fields are missing and distributions shift between markets.

LLM agents for high-stakes clinical workflows

In progress
Multi-agent systems · planning & tool use · RAG · guardrails and human-in-the-loop

Radiation therapy is a strictly serial pipeline where every stage contains a subjective human decision — which makes it an unusually clean testbed for agents. I mapped the full workflow to its decision points, surveyed the 2025–2026 agent literature layer by layer (general VLMs → general agents → medical VLMs → medical agents), and identified where autonomy is already crowded versus genuinely open.

  • A six-layer literature map tracing how failure modes compound downward: representation alignment → planning and tool reliability → domain shift → auditability and safety → closed-loop latency.
  • Comparative analysis of single-agent vs. multi-agent designs (CoT, RAG, RL-tuned parameter search, reviewer/validator roles) against real deployment constraints.
  • Focus on evaluation and safety: hallucination under domain shift, grounding, explicit safety checks, and auditable workflow decomposition — the parts that decide whether an agent ships.
  • The differentiating angle: coupling a domain-pretrained perception encoder to the agent's decision layer, rather than treating the agent as a domain-agnostic LLM wrapper.

Why it transfers: pluggable agent frameworks for task planning, tool use, and multi-turn interaction hit the same walls in e-commerce as in medicine — reliability, evaluation, and safety under real traffic. My interest is specifically in agents grounded in a learned representation, not in prompting alone.

Engineering

The infrastructure layer under the research: multi-fold training and evaluation pipelines for volumetric segmentation (OCT, brain tumor), promptable-segmentation adaptation with SAM2, and a benchmarking harness that keeps checkpoint selection, metric computation, significance testing, and figure generation reproducible across dozens of experimental configurations.

Why it transfers: most of research at scale is making comparisons trustworthy — fixed splits, controlled baselines, statistical significance, and an experiment log you can actually rerun.
